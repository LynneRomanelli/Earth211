# Earth211
Earth Science 211: Data Analysis in Earth and Environmental Sciences Final Project

Michigan Air Temperature by Population and Land Cover

This project examines Michigan air temperature data and its relationship with population and land cover. In addition to better understanding how Michigan air temperature has changed over time (including years between 1970 and 2025) and how it differs by county and peninsula, exploring the relationship between air temperature and population and land cover can help make sense of the factors that might impact air temperature. Better understanding how human population, developed land, vegetation, and water might affect air temperature is important because air temperature affects so many environmental processes and is a crucial part of examining global warming.

This project uses air temperature data from the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, land cover data from the 2020 Land Cover (CONUS) file from the National Land Cover Database, and population data from the U.S. Census Bureau and Michigan Statistical Abstract. This repository contains an R file with code for all the data analysis, statistical tests, and visualizations. There are also folders for raw data, processed data, figures, and the final report. 

After cleaning and compiling the air temperature, population, and land cover data, ANOVAs were run to compare air temperature across ten different years and across six land cover class categories. A Kruskal-Wallis test was used to compare air temperature across seventy counties in Michigan, and a t-test was run to compare air temperature between the upper and lower peninsula. Regressions and correlations were run to examine the relationships between air temperature and population and air temperature and proportion of land covered for each of the six land cover class categories.
